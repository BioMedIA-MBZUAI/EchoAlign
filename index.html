EchoAlign

A vision-language foundation model that aligns each cardiac structure, video and study with its report text, and restricts supervision to the views that actually depict each structure.

The problem ↓
02 · The problem

Each video shows only part of the heart. Its report describes all of it.

“Existing models are trained by pairing each video with the full clinical report, including structures and findings its view does not show. This mismatch lets a model associate a finding with incidental or spurious signals rather than its visual evidence.”

Across the 68 tasks, EchoAlign's mean AUROC is 0.890, the highest of the six models (P < 0.01 against each). It ranks first on 32 of the 68 tasks and on all eight regurgitation detection tasks.

Each finding is scored by the similarity of present and absent prompts to the anatomical study embedding of its own cardiac structure. Mean AUROC is 0.796, compared with 0.692 for EchoPrime and 0.692 for EchoCLIP; on the 39 tasks with a PanEcho head, 0.830 vs 0.828 for PanEcho's trained heads.

An ordinal probe grades each finding on its ordered scale. Mean QWK across 31 graded tasks is 0.530, the highest of the six models; EchoAlign ranks first on all six regurgitation grading tasks.

EchoAlign reaches a mean Dice of 0.880 on CAMUS, second to 0.892 for EchoFM, and 0.917 for the LV cavity on EchoNet-Dynamic. Across 25 measurement tasks, EchoPrime has the lowest RMSE on 11 tasks and EchoAlign on six.

On the same pretraining studies, mean AUROC rises from 0.815 to 0.869 for EchoAlign and from 0.725 to 0.803 for the EchoPrime recipe. With 1% of the probe labels, EchoAlign reaches 0.888 across 32 private tasks.
